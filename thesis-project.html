Problem

Clinicians miss alarms when attention is split.

ICU and OR staff face alarm fatigue, noisy environments, and monitors that are rarely in their direct line of sight. Critical alerts are delayed or overlooked, creating risk for patient safety.

Process

  1. Discover — contextual inquiry and expert consultation with clinicians.
  2. Define — translated insights into focus areas for attention and visibility.
  3. Ideate — sketched and prototyped AR alarm concepts with spatial cues.
  4. Build — developed an AR vital sign monitor prototype and data-collection interfaces.
  5. Test — evaluated with 23 participants in a controlled within-subjects study.

Proposal

Designed VitalViz: an AR-first vital sign display that keeps critical alarms visible in the clinician’s field of view using spatial position, color, and motion instead of relying on audio alone.

Research Question

Clinicians monitoring patients in the critical care rely on audio notifications and bedside monitors — but alarm fatigue and split attention between the patient and the monitor cause missed or delayed detection of critical alarms.

''An AR display that encodes alarm urgency spatially (rather than just visually or audibly) help clinicians detect and respond to critical alarms faster and more accurately, without adding to their cognitive load?''

Vital Sign Monitors

what are they anyway?

Physiological parameters such as blood pressure (BP), heart rate (HR), and oxygen saturation (SpO₂) are critical indicators of a patient’s health condition.

Vital signs monitors continuously display these vitals and issue visual and/ or auditory alarms to alert staff about health deterioration.

I might be out of the ICU, and don't have access to auditory alarms...

The anesthesia machine (with the patient monitor) is behind them and usually to the right..

The Problem

Alarm Fatigue & Noise Overload

ICUs and ORs are filled with constant alarms and conversations, which can overwhelm clinicians and disturb resting patients.

Limited Visibility & Accessibility

Sign monitors are often not in the clinician’s direct line of sight, causing delays and distraction due to constant shifts in focus between patient and screen.

UI Not optimized for Interpretation

The conventional format of physiological data representation (waveforms and numbers) requires high cognitive effort from clinicians. Several parameters can share the same or very similar values, which affects how different each variable appears.

My Solution

  • Each sphere represents a vital: HR (green), BP (red), and SpO₂ (blue).
  • Sole reliance on visuals to convey vital states without audio noise.
  • Leveraged AR's ability to overlay virtual objects anywhere in the clinician’s field of view.
  • Designed vitals to remain visible at all times, reducing distraction.

Methodology

Study Overview

Design
Within-subjects — AR display vs. conventional monitor; counterbalanced order
Participants
23 participants — mix of non-clinicians and nursing students / RNs; recruited at McGill University
Setting
Controlled lab environment with simulated patient scenarios and a visual workload task
1

Procedure

Participants completed both conditions while performing a distractor task (Fruit Ninja) to simulate visual workload. They monitored two simulated patients and paused to report any critical alarms. Conditions were trained and counterbalanced to reduce learning effects.

2

Measures

Primary outcomes: alarm detection accuracy and response time. Secondary measures: SUS usability score and post-study qualitative interviews to capture user experience and preferences.

Why this approach? Combining a simulated multitasking task with within-subjects design allowed us to directly compare alarm performance while controlling for individual differences.

Participants

23 participants — mixed expertise. Inclusion prioritized representation of intended end-users and naive users to evaluate learnability.

Data Analysis

Quantitative: GLME and ANOVA on accuracy and response times to control for learning and repeated measures. Qualitative: thematic analysis of interview transcripts.

Key Protocol Notes

  • Counterbalanced order to mitigate sequence effects
  • Standardized training for both conditions
  • Pause-and-report method for alarm detection

What these results meant..?

  • Higher alarm identification accuracy indicates that the AR display was easier to interpret and salient due to colors and movements.
  • Even though the response times were similar, the AR display has the potential to outperform a traditional monitor if it is further away from a clinician. Even in the current setup they had to move their heads back and forth.
  • Participants could multitask better while using the AR display because they did not have to divide their attention since the information was always visible to them.

My Learnings

The value of user feedback – Deep insights into the intricacies of their daily tasks and helped me design with real-world needs in mind.

Designing for emerging technology – Hands-on experience creating user interactions for AR applications using Unity and HoloLens.

Experiment setup matters – Attention to detail in designing the study setup is just as crucial as the application itself, to ensure valid results.

Unexpected insights from user studies – Even with a carefully crafted design, revealed surprising user requirements.
